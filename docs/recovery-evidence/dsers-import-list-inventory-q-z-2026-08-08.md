## Cluster finding

There is **no launch-ready, coherent low-risk cluster** in this Q–Z slice.

The most visually coherent cluster is travel packing/storage:

1. Travel Compression Packing Cubes — the only card with healthy stock;
2. Set/6 pcs Compressible Packing Cubes — stock 1 and missing weight/dimensions;
3. 3Pcs Compressible Packing Cubes — stock 10.

That is not a useful three-product architecture: the items are near-duplicates, two cannot support demand, and their set/variant structures invite fulfillment and customer-expectation errors. Do not promote this as a catalog cluster.

The two lowest physical-risk concepts are the Toocki cable clips and wardrobe grid organizer, but they do not form a coherent customer mission by themselves and both retain documentary/stock problems. The under-sink products are not low risk because size/fit, bulk, weight, configuration complexity, and returns dominate.

## Recommendation

- **Do not push any Q–Z Import List card solely from this inventory evidence.** Route and repeatable landed-cost proof remains outstanding by design.
- If a later shortlist needs one Q–Z follow-up, inspect the 13-variant Travel Compression card first because it alone has meaningful stock; constrain it to one exact set/color before any mapping decision.
- Treat the Toocki card only as a passive accessory lead, not an approved product, until branding/authorization and attachment-surface limitations are documented.
- Deprioritize both under-sink entries and the two low-stock packing-cube cards without spending route-audit time.
