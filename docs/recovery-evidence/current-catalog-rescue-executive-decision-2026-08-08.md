# Current-catalog rescue: executive decision

**Decision date:** 2026-08-08
**Scope:** current catalog only; evidence consolidation, not a catalog mutation
**Operating constraints:** dropshipping remains the model; no purchase/sample requirement; no ads; no new sourcing in this decision

## Binding decision

**The current catalog has no launch cohort. Do not launch, advertise, remap, or rebuild the storefront around it.**

The three cluster audits all returned **NO-GO**. More importantly, the few economically plausible leads solve unrelated customer problems: a milk frother, a magnetic car charger, and a water flosser do not form a niche, bundle, acquisition story, or credible three-product collection. Treating them as a general-store launch would repeat the catalog-churn failure this rescue was meant to stop.

No current lead should anchor a niche.

## Why DSers sales did not cure the mappings

The DSers `Sale` figure was useful for ranking visual-match rows, but it was never an approval gate:

1. Supplier Optimizer returned **image matches**, not proof that a row was the exact product, option or supplier mapped to Shopify. Several promising IDs differed from the supplier-style IDs embedded in current variants.
2. The inspected rows frequently omitted the selected option, option-level stock, exact title, configuration, contents and tracking. DSers itself warned that displayed data may be incorrect.
3. A high count does not resolve product risk. VGR showed `439` yet lacked authenticity/resale and exact-kit proof; the straightener showed `348` yet lacked heated-lithium safety/configuration proof; and a `359`-sale hair result was a crimper/waver, not the advertised 2-in-1 tool.
4. Sales cannot repair a contradiction such as an EU 220–240V plug for Canada/U.S., a button feeder sold with WiFi copy, an unverified branded product, or a variant range that includes loss-making options.

Therefore, **DSers sales is a discovery signal only**. Approval still requires the exact supplier ID + exact selected option + repeatable ordinary item cost + option stock + complete configuration/contents + viable CA and U.S. route + truthful claims, all for the same SKU.

## One recommended operational path

**End the current-catalog rescue and keep the storefront/catalog quarantined. The next authorized product cycle should be a supplier-first passive pet-hair reset—not another salvage or broad-product search.**

This is a single gated path:

1. Preserve the three HOLDs above as research notes only; do not spend more launch work on them and do not replace a failed product with another unrelated current-catalog item.
2. When sourcing is authorized again, begin with the previously identified passive pet-hair problem/offer and validate suppliers **before** any Shopify product, page, creative or navigation work: exact IDs/options, current stock, repeatable ordinary landed cost to Canada and the U.S., tracked ETA, materials/surface limitations, contents and product-specific review evidence.
3. Require a coherent three-product pet-hair cohort to pass the same gate before storefront work resumes. The existing two-item “Pet Hair Rescue Duo” remains a hypothesis, not an approved offer; its combined all-in landed cost must remain at or below the established **CA$12** ceiling for a CA$39–44 offer.
4. If the passive cohort cannot clear the gate, stop. Do not fill the gap with electronics, branded goods, oral-contact items, heated/battery devices, or more visual-match HOLDs.

Passive pet-hair remains the evidence-backed fallback because it is demonstrable, compact, unbranded and avoids the app, plug/voltage, battery, thermal, oral-contact and brand-authorization failure modes that defeated the current catalog. This recommendation does **not** require ordering a sample and does not change the dropshipping model. It changes the sequence: supplier truth first, catalog second.
