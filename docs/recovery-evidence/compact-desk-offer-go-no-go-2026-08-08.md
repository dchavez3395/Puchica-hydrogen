Scope: smaller/lighter hero alternatives to clamp-on metal trays
Decision: **NO-GO for paid acquisition; conditional organic validation only**
Mutation boundary: evidence only; nothing imported

## Executive decision

A credible CA$60–75 compact-desk bundle has **not** been assembled. The two remaining public leads have no exact DSers Canada/US route, exact selected SKU, destination shipping, or recent demand proof. A comparable two-slot aluminum stand is already visible at Walmart Canada for **CA$39.80**, so even a future bundle would face a low visible price ceiling.

This is not a rejection of the customer problem. It is a rejection of spending ads on an unproven, easily price-compared bundle.

## Rejected bundle attempt

Candidate `1005011982937617` is excluded. Its ordinary/reference price is **US$11.59**, above the established cable-routing ceiling, while the offer contains only six clips. More importantly, the sourcing rules reject magnets near critical desk devices. It must not be used to make the stand-and-routing concept appear differentiated or economical.

The remaining stand and tie-roll leads are not a complete offer. Their public item prices sum to **CA$17.53 before shipping**, but that subtotal is not a landed cost, neither item has an exact DSers Canada/US route, and the tie roll cannot supply enough perceived value to turn the stand into a defensible CA$60–75 bundle.

### Why the differentiation is insufficient today

- “A system, not one gadget” is sound merchandising, but no acceptable system has been sourced.
- The stand has compatibility risk, while the tie roll is a low-value commodity.
- No exact candidate exposes sufficient recent order/review evidence publicly.
- Neither lead has an exact DSers Canada/US route or selected-SKU proof.
- The transformation is useful, but less emotionally urgent and less visually dramatic than a strong cleaning problem.

## GO / NO-GO rule

**Current verdict: NO-GO for ads.** Do not import this as a finished launch offer and do not spend acquisition money on it.

The desk concept remains NO-GO. The two leads may be inspected only to close the evidence gap; that inspection does not make a bundle viable. At minimum DSers would need to prove:

1. Exact selected SKUs and ordinary prices, with no welcome-deal assumptions.
2. Canada and US routes, destination shipping, and delivery promise for each exact SKU.
3. A separate non-magnetic, within-ceiling routing component before any bundle can be reconsidered.
4. Stand slot range, supported device thickness, padding, dimensions, and stability.
5. Supplier/listing history with meaningful recent orders and reviews.
6. Fresh landed-cost and competitive-price math for the newly assembled system.

No retail test price is authorized from the present evidence.
